In the open-ended section, we wanted to go beyond just adding or removing techniques from the pipeline and instead understand what really drives the model’s performance. For that, we ran an ablation study to measure the impact of each feature and check whether the effect is consistent across cross-validation folds.

We trained a baseline model using all features, then created variants where we remove one feature at a time (“w/o feature”) and evaluated them with K-fold cross-validation. We used RMSE as the main metric (lower is better). To avoid drawing conclusions from random fold variation, we also compared each variant to the baseline with paired significance tests and applied Holm correction for multiple comparisons. We then ranked features by how much RMSE worsens when they are removed and summarized the results with plots.

The results show a clear importance ranking. Removing engineSize and model causes the biggest and statistically significant RMSE increase, making them the strongest drivers. Removing year, mileage, and mpg also leads to consistent, significant drops in performance. In contrast, tax, Brand, hasDamage, and fuelType have small and mostly non-significant effects, so they add limited value in this setup. Interestingly, removing transmission and paintQuality% slightly improves RMSE, which suggests these features may be noisy or not well represented in the current model. Overall, this ablation study complements the benchmarking/tuning work by clearly showing which features matter most and which ones are worth revisiting.
